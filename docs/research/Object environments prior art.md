# Copy Foundry's ideas, plug in Postgres parts

No single open-source system combines objects, gated actions, a Postgres blackboard and harnesses joining through a model proxy, so Substrate's core is new and the surrounding parts can mostly be plugged in or copied. The closest Foundry-like projects are not adoptable: OpenFoundry's GitHub repo returned 404 on 2026-11-23 and its licence is disputed (Apache-2.0 on its docs, AGPL-3.0 on a Gitee mirror), and Semantica (MIT, 14.7k stars) covers only the knowledge-graph half, with no actions or permissions. The sound pieces are PostgREST (MIT), pgmq (PostgreSQL licence) and Postgres roles with row-level security for permissions. For standards, W3C Thing Description 1.1 maps almost one-to-one to properties, actions and events, MCP is the joining surface, and OpenEnv can be a thin adapter. BDI with LLMs is not proven: the 2024-2026 evidence is prototype-scale, but it supplies a pattern worth copying, which is an LLM proposing into a typed vocabulary, a validator filtering, and an explicit layer owning commitment and retraction. On the business side, Foundry costs six to seven figures a year, and nothing sourced shows sub-enterprise buyers purchasing "ontology" as such. What they pay for is AI implementation help, so the first paid offer should be a fixed-price, five-day "process map plus one gated automation" pilot at roughly $2,500-$7,500, credited against a build. First cash within a week is unlikely; one to four weeks is realistic.

## BDI with LLMs is a useful pattern, not a proven structure

The 2024-2026 literature is small and mostly workshop-level. It falls into three patterns. In the first, an LLM is a natural-language front end to a BDI agent: ChatBDI keeps Jason doing the thinking while a model embeds messages and maps them to the closest KQML literal, and it does not generate plans ([ChatBDI](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2025/pdfs/p2541.pdf)). In the second, an LLM writes plans into the BDI plan library. Ciatto et al. (ECAI 2025) extend AgentSpeak(L) with a plan-generation procedure that runs on demand, after plan failure or proactively, with failed plans retracted ([paper](https://cris.unibo.it/bitstream/11585/1026414/3/FAIA-413-FAIA251223.pdf)). Jarvis (WESAAC 2026) introspects CArtAgO artifacts' operation signatures and observable properties, asks an LLM for an AgentSpeak library, and validates arity and subgoals before injecting plans ([Jarvis](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/wesaac/article/download/45248/45009/)). In the third, an LLM sits inside a rule-based BDI shell for believability, as in a child-helpline trainer ([arXiv 2509.16784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16784)).

The measured evidence is thin. Ciatto's one quantified result covers a single grid-world task, 4 models and 5 runs each, and success ranged from **100% for the best model down to 20% for the worst**, so BDI structure does not remove the dependence on the planner model. The Utah self-aware BDI agent scored 13.2% (any round, simulation mode) and 9.4% in the seeded and non-seeded single-pass runs on 53 GAIA questions with Qwen3-8B, and plan seeding did not improve accuracy ([UUCS-26-001](https://www-old.cs.utah.edu/docs/techreports/2026/UUCS-26-001.pdf)). The researcher found no head-to-head showing BDI+LLM beating a plain tool-calling agent. No 2024-2026 system compiles LLM episodes into plans Soar-style with measured results. That absence is weak evidence of novelty, since Soar literature was outside the search scope, but it means a "System 1 distilled from experience" has no BDI-side proof to lean on. What plan libraries demonstrably give is structure, auditability and cheap reuse: Jarvis spent about 1.5K input and 200 output tokens once per learned plan.

The practical advice is to hand-roll the cycle rather than adopt a runtime. Jason (v3.3.3, 2026-10-15) and JaCaMo (v1.3.1) are LGPL-3.0 and alive, but they are JVM-only with no maintained Python binding ([Jason](https://github.com/jason-lang/jason), [JaCaMo](https://github.com/jacamo-lang/jacamo)). CArtAgO reports GPL-3.0 through the GitHub API with its last push in July 2024, so it could only run as a separate service ([CArtAgO](https://api.github.com/repos/CArtAgO-lang/cartago)). Of the Python options, MASPY (MIT, pushed 2026-11-13) is the one to read for semantics, but its runtime owns its own belief store ([MASPY](https://api.github.com/repos/laca-is/MASPY)). Belief, goal and intention tables with the plan library as rows in the shared Postgres fit the blackboard better. Copy four things: the typed vocabulary and validator before a plan enters the library, failure-driven retraction, artifact signatures as grounding, and recording which model wrote each plan.

## Foundry's model is six ideas, and no open project packages them

Palantir describes the Ontology as an operational layer with a semantic half (objects, properties, links) and a kinetic half (actions, functions, dynamic security) ([Palantir overview](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/overview/)). An action is "a single transaction" composed of rules, parameters, submission criteria, permissions and side effects ([action types](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/action-types/overview/)). Agents reach this through an Ontology MCP that exposes object types, action types and query functions as tools, restricted by application scopes ([OMCP](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology-mcp/overview/)). The central idea to copy is that agents write only through actions, never raw tables. Palantir's facts here are second-hand, because they were read through a summarising fetch tool.

The projects that market themselves as alternatives are weak. OpenFoundry (Go, 41 microservices per its Gitee mirror) has the right design, with validate, execute, batch-execute and what-if endpoints on actions, but the repo is gone and the licence conflicts ([Gitee mirror](https://gitee.com/zdy336/OpenFoundry)). Semantica has real adoption and an MCP server but stores graphs in Neo4j, FalkorDB, Apache AGE and similar stores, with no relational Postgres store and no described access control ([Semantica](https://github.com/Hawksight-AI/semantica)). The ibl.ai "agentic ontology" is a vendor claim whose guessed repo does not exist, and agent-utilities (now meristem, MIT, 5 stars) shows no verified ontology layer ([meristem](https://github.com/Knuckles-Team/meristem)). The popular "open-source Palantir" write-ups are claims, not evidence.

The adjacent systems split by licence, which is the filter that matters for Substrate's rules.

| System | Licence | Use |
|---|---|---|
| PostgREST | MIT | Plug in: HTTP/RPC over Postgres functions, roles and RLS |
| pgmq | PostgreSQL | Already in use |
| Supabase | Apache-2.0 | Copy the grants + RLS pattern |
| Hasura v2 | Apache-2.0 | Copy actions and event triggers |
| Odoo | LGPL-3 | Richest ready-made object/action/ACL model, but a monolith |
| OpenMetadata, DataHub, Atlas | Apache-2.0 | Catalogs describe objects but do not act on them |
| TypeDB | MPL-2.0 | Strong typed schema, separate service, not Postgres |
| Neo4j Community | GPL-3.0 | Separate process only |
| Directus, NocoDB, SpacetimeDB | source-available (MSCL, Sustainable Use, BSL 1.1) | Fail the default licence test |
| Twenty, Budibase | AGPL / GPL with paid parts | Separate service at best |

Sources: [PostgREST, Supabase, Hasura, Odoo, Directus and others as tabulated in the research notes via GitHub API and LICENSE files](https://github.com/directus/directus/blob/main/license); [SpacetimeDB licence](https://www.nuget.org/packages/SpacetimeDB.Runtime/2.8.0/License). Licence fields were read from the GitHub API on 2026-11-23. Wikibase's licence text was not read.

Postgres RLS as the agent permission model is verified in practice: Supabase runs requests under the caller's role ([Supabase RLS](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security)), and pgEdge's MCP server maps each user to a Postgres role ([pgEdge](https://docs.pgedge.com/pgedge-postgres-mcp-server/v1-0-0/advanced/row-level-security/)). RLS covers only SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE and does not solve prompt injection. The inferred cheap pattern is that an object type is a table or `kind` rows with a JSON schema, a link is an edge table, an action is a SQL function run as the caller's role that writes an event row and a pgmq message, and the MCP tool list is generated from the action registry. No source states this pattern; it is a design inference.

## Standards fit as thin adapters, and the gate is the gap

W3C Thing Description 1.1 (Recommendation, 5 Dec 2023) defines properties, actions, events, forms and security schemes, a near-exact match to the object model ([TD 1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/wot-thing-description11/)). TD 2.0 is only a First Public Working Draft from Nov 2025 ([TD 2.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/2025/WD-wot-thing-description-2.0-20251104)). The free/propose/deny gate has no native TD field and would be an extension term. Generating TDs from the object tables is cheap; node-wot (EPL-2.0 or W3C licence) is an optional runtime needing Node in a container ([node-wot](https://github.com/eclipse-thingweb/node-wot)).

Agents and Artifacts (CArtAgO) is the closest academic precedent: artifacts with operations, observable properties and signals live in workspaces, and agents "focus" on the ones they care about ([Ricci et al. 2011](https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/108512)). Copy the vocabulary: focus as an explicit subscription row, state (replayable) versus signal (transient), and operations that can fail with feedback. A&A has no permission gate and its artifacts are code classes rather than trait-based data. The A&A detail rests on secondary sources, because the official docs and licence were not fetched.

OpenEnv (BSD-3, "early development") is an episodic Gym-style protocol with reset, step and state over WebSocket, and MCP tools at `/mcp` ([OpenEnv](https://github.com/meta-pytorch/OpenEnv)). Its production mode serves MCP tools with no episode control ([HF docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/openenv/main/en/guides/simulation-vs-production)), and RFC 005 (still In Review) wraps an unmodified Claude Code through a localhost MCP bridge ([RFC 005](https://github.com/huggingface/OpenEnv/blob/main/rfcs/005-agentic-harnesses.md)). Its Harbor/RFC 006 route records every model call through a proxy to capture token traces ([HF harnesses](https://huggingface.co/docs/openenv/main/en/tutorials/harnesses)), which is the same idea as Substrate's gateway and a ready format for training a System 1. Spec churn is real: repo locations and RFC numbers disagree across sources, and it has no free/propose/deny notion. A single live database also does not fit `reset` without a per-episode schema or branch. Adopt through an adapter and do not build the core around it.

MCP is the joining surface. The 2025-11-25 spec gives tools, URI-identified resources, subscriptions and annotations ([spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/server/resources)). The 2026-07-28 revision, whose final or draft status was not confirmed, removes sessions and replaces resource subscribe with `subscriptions/listen`, and adds `input_required` results ([changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog)). Statelessness suits a database-as-state design, and `input_required` is a natural carrier for the `propose` gate (inference). Beyond these, CloudEvents (CNCF graduated, 1.0.2) is a cheap envelope for events in pgmq and the record ([CloudEvents](https://github.com/cloudevents/spec)), and A2A v1.0 task states (INPUT_REQUIRED, WORKING, COMPLETED, REJECTED) map to a proposed action's lifecycle ([A2A](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/)). Home Assistant's stable `unique_id` and availability conventions are worth copying ([HA docs](https://contributors.home-assistant.io/core/entities/entity-class/)). CUBE and Open Agent Spec are young and best ignored except as export targets. A 2026 survey of three harnesses finds that tamper-evident external verifiability is the one axis where they have not converged, which supports the append-only record ([arXiv 2608.23953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23953)).

The researcher found no system combining database-as-environment, gated object actions and harnesses joining via a model proxy. Absence of search hits is weak evidence; GitHub was not searched for Letta, LangGraph-on-Postgres or pgmq-based agent frameworks, which could be partial precedents.

## Buyers pay for implementation help, and Foundry pricing is out of reach

Adoption is broad and shallow. About 76% of small businesses use AI but only **14% have fully integrated it into core operations** ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2026/small-businesses-embrace-ai-but-need-training-and-support-to-fully-harness-it); the page returned 403 so figures come from a search summary). Thryv's April 2026 survey of 561 decision-makers found 66% usage and 70% wanting more training ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/07/14/ai-adoption-continues-to-rise-but-70-say-they-need-more-training/186565/)). MSPs see the demand but lack delivery capacity: 48% rank AI as the top client need, and only 13% earn meaningful AI revenue ([ITPro/Kaseya](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/ai-demand-soars-as-traditional-deal-sizes-shrink-kaseya-reports), a vendor-affiliated source). No evidence shows a sub-enterprise segment buying "ontology" by name, and no named solo seller with prices was found.

Palantir publishes no prices. Estimates run from $800K-$2.5M a year for a mid-market enterprise (a negotiation consultancy, an interested party) ([Redress](https://www.redresscompliance.com/palantir-aip-foundry-negotiation.html)) to a single AWS Marketplace listing at $100,000 per month for one month ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/B0B1P1GDTF)). No transacted prices were found. The relevant band for a solo is AI-automation agency pricing, per vendor blogs with 2-4x disagreement: $2,000-$15,000 setup plus $500-$5,000 a month ([Digital Agency Network](https://digitalagencynetwork.com/ai-agency-pricing/)), and readiness audits at $2,500-$10,000 ([AI Smart Ventures](https://aismartventures.com/posts/what-should-an-ai-readiness-assessment-include-and-what-should-you-pay-for-one-a-practical-buyers-guide-for-owner-operated-businesses)), some crediting the fee against a build. Marketplaces are a poor channel: UpHunt's analysis of Aug-Oct 2026 jobs puts AI-automation medians at $15-$30 an hour with 7.1% reaching $100 or more ([UpHunt](https://uphunt.io/blog/upwork-ai-consultant-hourly-rate-2026-data)). Conversion evidence is anecdotal, such as one consultancy reaching $4,000 a month after five months on about $4K setup plus $500 a month ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/soratech/after-5-months-my-business-hit-4000-month-revenue-and-8000-total-revenue--N_yAWwJP1-mhVVs1_LQ)).

Privacy helps the pitch but self-hosting does not obviously sell. Only 23% of SMBs have a documented AI policy ([SecureWorld](https://www.secureworld.io/industry-news/smb-ai-governance-security)), and top challenges are missing expertise (45%) and data governance (41%) ([ERP Today](https://erp.today/sage-idc-research-reveals-smb-cybersecurity-gaps-in-the-ai-era/)). Permission gates, human approval and an append-only record answer those fears directly. No survey shows SMBs asking for local LLMs, and a handover leaves them without operators, so a hosted-by-you offer with a data-handling statement is likelier to close.

## Recommendation: plug in, copy, build, and sell

Plug in PostgREST (MIT) as the HTTP layer over the action functions, pgmq for queues, Postgres roles and RLS for permissions, and MCP as the agent surface; the kernel's MCP profile already follows this. Add a thin OpenEnv production-mode adapter and TD generation only when a buyer or training run needs them. Copy Palantir's six ideas, with action validate/execute/what-if and audit from OpenFoundry's documented design, A&A's focus and state-versus-signal split, A2A task states, CloudEvents envelopes and Home Assistant's stable ids. Copy the BDI discipline (typed vocabulary, validator, failure-driven retraction) as tables and gate checks, and skip Jason, CArtAgO and SPADE runtimes. What looks genuinely new, with the caveat that search absence is weak evidence, is the combination: trait-based objects whose actions sit behind free/propose/deny gates in one Postgres that is blackboard, bus, auth and record, with unmodified harnesses joining through a model proxy that records every call. A System 1 distilled from that record has no proven precedent in BDI work, so treat it as research with its own measurements.

The first paid offer is a **five-day fixed-price "map and one gated automation" pilot** for owner-operated firms of 10-200 people, priced at $2,500-$7,500 and credited against a build, then a $500-$2,000 monthly retainer for hosting and approvals. Deliver the decomposed map (objects, roles, interfaces) as the audit, then one automation whose writes go through a propose gate with a human approval step, hosted by you. Sell visibility, fewer manual steps and approvals, and describe it as AI automation, not an ontology. Reach 5-10 known firms directly, and approach 2-3 local MSPs about white-label delivery, since they have demand and no capacity. This follows Palantir's own five-day bootcamp pattern at small scale ([Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/index.php/insights/news/palantirs-commercial-business-scales-help-ai-boot-camps)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the build question from "what Foundry clone do we adopt" to "which commodity parts do we let carry the weight", because the open Foundry alternatives are either gone, source-available or only half the model, while the Postgres parts (PostgREST, pgmq, RLS) are mature and permissive. The real contribution is the gate plus the record: standards such as TD, A&A, MCP and OpenEnv each cover affordances or transport but none covers propose-and-approve, and the harness literature itself names tamper-evident verification as unsolved.

Several uncertainties remain open. BDI plan distillation has no measured benefit yet, so System 1 claims should be tested rather than assumed. The MCP 2026-07-28 status, OpenEnv's governance and CArtAgO's licence are unverified, and every price in the market section is an asking price from vendor or agency writing. The pilot also has no sourced conversion rate, so its first sales are the experiment that fills that gap.
