# Market below enterprise scale for Foundry/AIP-like platforms, and fast first-revenue routes for a solo developer

Method note: ~12 searches/fetches, 2026-11-23. Several fetches returned 403/404 (Goldman Sachs release, one dev.to article), so those items rest on search-result summaries. Most pricing sources are vendor or agency blogs (asking prices, not transacted prices). Flagged where so.

## Who buys Foundry/AIP-like capability below enterprise scale, and what problems do they pay to solve?

### Takeaway
SMBs are adopting AI broadly but shallowly: most use AI tools, few have integrated AI into core operations, and most say they need training and implementation help. That implementation gap, not a lack of tools, is the paid problem. I found no evidence of a sub-enterprise buyer segment purchasing "ontology" as such; demand is expressed as AI/automation and as MSP client need.

### Cited Findings
- Thryv survey (April 2026, 561 US SMB decision-makers): AI usage 66%, up from 55% a year earlier; 70% say they need more training — [CPA Practice Advisor, 2026-07-14](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/07/14/ai-adoption-continues-to-rise-but-70-say-they-need-more-training/186565/)
- Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses (Jan–Feb 2026, 1,256 owners): about 76% use AI, 93% report positive impact, but only 14% have fully integrated AI into core operations; headline says they need training and support — [Goldman Sachs press release 2026](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2026/small-businesses-embrace-ai-but-need-training-and-support-to-fully-harness-it) (page itself returned 403; figures from the search summary and title, verify before quoting)
- Intuit QuickBooks AI Impact Report: 77% regular use as of Jan 2026 (US, Canada, UK, Australia SMBs) — [Intuit](https://www.intuit.com/blog/global-stories/ai-impact-report/)
- Survey figures span ~40% to 87% depending on definition; Census BTOS uses a strict production-based definition and is lower — same search summary; sources include [Bluehost](https://www.bluehost.com/state-of-small-business-ai-confidence/ai-confidence-divide), [SF Fed](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/community-development-research-briefs/2026/07/ai-adoption-in-small-businesses-2024-sbcs/)
- US Chamber Foundation: half of small-business workers use AI mostly to boost productivity, not automate jobs — [US Chamber Foundation](https://www.uschamberfoundation.org/workforce/half-of-small-business-workers-use-ai-most-to-boost-productivity-not-automate-jobs)
- MSPs (the IT firms serving SMBs): Kaseya 2026 State of the MSP (1,000+ providers) — 48% rank AI and automation as top client need for 2026, but only 13% currently earn meaningful revenue from it — [ITPro/Kaseya summary](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/ai-demand-soars-as-traditional-deal-sizes-shrink-kaseya-reports); [Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/ai/msp-ai-revenue-growth-2026/). Vendor-affiliated source.
- Informa MSP 501 (600+ MSPs): about one-third expect AI revenue to grow more than 20% in 2026 — [Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/ai/msp-ai-revenue-growth-2026/)
- Palantir's own framing: AIP boot camps deliver working workflows on the customer's own data in 5 days or less; planned for 140+ organizations by end of November (2024) — [Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/index.php/insights/news/palantirs-commercial-business-scales-help-ai-boot-camps)

### Inferences
- The buyer to target is the owner-operated or 10–200 person firm that already uses ChatGPT-type tools and cannot connect them to its own data and processes; an MSP serving many such firms lacks capacity for it, so MSPs are a channel as well as a client type.
- "Ontology" is a delivery mechanism, not a purchase reason. Sell the outcome (visibility, fewer manual steps, approvals), not the model.

### Gaps
- No usable data on local government or nonprofit spend on Foundry-like tools (not searched deeply; no results surfaced).
- No data on how many SMBs buy "operations visibility/data unification" specifically versus generic AI.

## Price points 2025-2026 (Palantir, mid-market alternatives, agency/consulting rates)

### Takeaway
Palantir is priced far above any solo-developer client: published signals are six to seven figures a year. The relevant market for a solo is the AI-automation agency band: roughly $2k–$15k setup plus $500–$5k/month retainer, and $2.5k–$10k for fixed-fee audits, per vendor-written guides that disagree by 2–4x.

### Cited Findings
- Palantir publishes no prices; CostBench lists AIP as custom enterprise-only pricing — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-ml-platforms/palantir-aip/)
- Negotiation consultancy Redress Compliance estimates $800K–$2.5M annual platform fees for a mid-market enterprise (interested party, directional) — [Redress Compliance](https://www.redresscompliance.com/palantir-aip-foundry-negotiation.html)
- Spendflo: Foundry typically $100K to several million per year (weak source); an AWS Marketplace listing shows a Foundry Subscription Unit at $100,000 per month on a one-month contract (a listing example, not a typical deal) — [Spendflo](https://spendflo.com/vendors/foundry), [AWS Marketplace](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/B0B1P1GDTF)
- Bootcamp price disputed: one review blog says about $1M starting (unverified); Palantir's annual report reportedly describes low or no-cost evaluation pilots with bootcamps — [reviewaitool](https://blog.reviewaitool.com/?p=186), [Bloomberg 2024-04-23](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-04-23/inside-palantir-s-ai-sales-secret-weapon-software-boot-camp)
- Per-seat mid-market tools for contrast: Retool Team $10/mo, Business $50/mo; Airtable Plus $10, Pro $20 (per-seat presumed, aggregator data, may be stale) — [Findstack](https://findstack.com/compare/retool-vs-airtable). Alteryx entry $3,000/user/year — [G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/alteryx-vs-palantir-foundry)
- Agency retainers and setup (vendor blogs; asking prices): small-business retainer from ~$500/mo; light retainer $1,000–$3,500/mo; hybrid $2,000–$8,000 setup + $1,500–$4,000/mo; wider $2,500–$15,000 setup + $500–$5,000/mo; mid-market setup $20,000+ — [DEV Community guide](https://dev.to/scalelogix_ai/how-to-price-ai-agency-services-in-2026-a-complete-guide-for-operators-5f26), [Digital Agency Network](https://digitalagencynetwork.com/ai-agency-pricing/), [Taskip](https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/). Integration complexity is the largest cost driver.
- AI readiness audit: typical $2,500–$10,000 for a small business; a 10-day audit from $7,500 fixed; one provider credits the 3–5 day assessment fee against a build; UK guide £750–£8,000 for firms under 50 staff; enterprise tier $15K–$50K+ — [AI Smart Ventures buyer guide](https://aismartventures.com/posts/what-should-an-ai-readiness-assessment-include-and-what-should-you-pay-for-one-a-practical-buyers-guide-for-owner-operated-businesses), [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/ai-audit-small-business-cost/). Timeline 1–3 weeks; client staff time 15–20 hours.
- Marketplace gig tiers (Upwork productized AI audit): $150 / $400 / $900 at 3 / 7 / 14 days — [Upwork listing](https://www.upwork.com/services/product/development-it-an-ai-consulting-audit-and-roadmap-for-your-small-business-2075758213237450880)
- Upwork client-posted budgets: UpHunt's analysis of Aug–Oct 2026 jobs — AI automation jobs (n8n, Make, Zapier) median $15–$30/hr, only 7.1% reach $100+/hr; Upwork says AI-skilled freelancer GSV grew 55% y/y in Q3 2026 (quoted by UpHunt) — [UpHunt](https://uphunt.io/blog/upwork-ai-consultant-hourly-rate-2026-data). Contradicted by blog claims of $150–$350/hr for US independents ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/jahanzaibai/ai-automation-consultant-hourly-rate-what-youll-actually-pay-in-2026-and-when-its-worth-it-55ln), unverified).
- Palantir negotiation note: pilots begun without agreed commercial terms converted at materially worse pricing — [Redress Compliance](https://redresscompliance.com/palantir-aip-foundry-negotiation) (interested party).

### Inferences
- Realistic first-sale range: $2,500–$7,500 fixed for audit/pilot, then a $500–$2,000/mo retainer. Marketplace bidding competes with $15–$30/hr automators; direct outreach avoids that.
- Palantir's 5-day boot camp on the customer's data is the model to copy at small scale: a fixed-length, fixed-price, credited-against-build pilot.

### Gaps
- No verified transacted prices (Vendr/Vertice Palantir data was not accessible). No pricing found for Dust, Glean or other agent platforms. No sourced day rate for solo ontology consultants.

## Fast first-revenue routes and evidence of conversion

### Takeaway
Evidence on conversion is mostly anecdote. The recurring pattern is a narrow niche, a fixed-scope audit or pilot, one working automation, and a small retainer. Nothing found proves a one-week timeline; sourced examples are weeks to months.

### Cited Findings
- Indie Hackers, Soratech (AI/workflow automation consultancy, 2023 post, dated): roughly $4K setup plus $500/mo retainer; $4,000/mo and $8,000 total revenue after 5 months — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/soratech/after-5-months-my-business-hit-4000-month-revenue-and-8000-total-revenue--N_yAWwJP1-mhVVs1_LQ)
- Indie Hackers anecdote: first $1,000 from a lead-generation agency client wanting a custom AI app — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-i-made-my-first-1-000-from-a-single-client-by-listing-my-email-on-a-free-tool-site-no-ads-no-cold-emails-2b2d349ee1)
- Indie Hackers anecdote: first paying customer closed only after 6 pitches were ignored — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-i-closed-my-first-paying-customer-after-6-pitches-got-ignored-17ec2087bf)
- Pattern advice (promotional, unverified): narrow niche; scoped free audit ending in one working agent; consulting first, productize later — [dev.to](https://dev.to/michael_xero_ai/how-to-get-your-first-ai-consulting-client-without-a-portfolio-4n1h), [MindStudio](https://mindstudio.ai/blog/start-ai-automation-business-case-studies). Several results were sales pages for courses.
- MSP partnership: Pax8 is pushing agentic AI offers to MSPs to build recurring revenue; MSPs lack delivery capacity (13% earning meaningful AI revenue) — [ChannelE2E](https://www.channele2e.com/news/pax8-wants-msps-to-turn-smb-ai-demand-into-managed-services). Counterpoint: reselling AI is not where MSP margins are made — [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/why-reselling-ai-isnt-where-msp-margins-are-made)

### Inferences
- Fastest realistic route this week: (1) a paid fixed-price "process map and one automation pilot" sold by direct outreach to 5–10 known small firms, (2) a white-label delivery offer to 2–3 local MSPs, (3) a marketplace productized listing as a low-priced lead source (expect low budgets). Expect first cash in 1–4 weeks, not guaranteed within one.
- Use the mapping tool as the audit deliverable (it is the differentiator: a decomposed map with interfaces), with the pilot credited against the build.

### Gaps
- No sourced conversion rates (outreach to meeting to sale), no verified sales-cycle length for sub-$10K AI projects. Open-core and marketplace-product revenue for this category: no evidence found.

## What buyers fear, and how self-hosted/local-model positioning helps or hurts

### Takeaway
Data privacy and lack of AI security expertise are top SMB concerns, which supports a privacy-first pitch, but I found no survey showing SMBs ask for self-hosting or local LLMs; they mostly look for certifications and transparency.

### Cited Findings
- Pax8 survey (1,150 SMB leaders): data security and privacy a top AI risk concern for over one-third; over one-third found unapproved shadow AI in six months; Q2 2026 pulse: 23% have a documented AI policy, 28% informal only — [SecureWorld](https://www.secureworld.io/industry-news/smb-ai-governance-security), [ChannelDive](https://www.channeldive.com/news/smbs-lack-ai-governance-report/832804/)
- IDC/Sage: top AI security challenges are insufficient internal expertise (45%) and implementing data governance (41%); certifications and data-handling transparency are the main trust mechanisms — [ERP Today](https://erp.today/sage-idc-research-reveals-smb-cybersecurity-gaps-in-the-ai-era/)
- Kaseya: accuracy and data privacy slow adoption — [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/business/business-strategy/kaseya-smbs-remain-cautious-on-ai-despite-persistent-human-error-threat)
- Proton SMB report digest: 45% unclear how AI firms handle data; 33% cite lack of deletion guarantees (fragmentary, unverified) — [SecurityBrief](https://securitybrief.news/story/smbs-take-cautious-approach-to-ai-adoption-study-finds)

### Inferences
- Helps: permission-gated actions, human approval, provenance and local models answer governance and shadow-AI fears; sell "AI policy plus controlled agent" for regulated or privacy-sensitive niches (legal, health, accounting, local government).
- Hurts: self-hosting means the buyer needs someone to operate it; a solo developer is a single point of failure, and SMBs lack internal expertise. A hosted-by-you managed option with a data-handling statement is likelier to close than a handover.

### Gaps
- No survey data on SMB demand for self-hosted or local LLMs; no data on reliability fears about solo vendors.

## Examples of solo founders or small firms selling ontology/knowledge-graph/agent systems to SMBs

### Takeaway
I found no documented solo seller of an ontology product to SMBs with a stated price. Evidence is limited to generic AI automation consultancies and marketplace listings.

### Cited Findings
- Soratech (above): $4K setup + $500/mo, automations and bots — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/soratech)
- PeoplePerHour listing frames a minimum-viable-ontology consultation: domain entities and relationships, design for priority queries, graph DB recommendation, phased roadmap (price not captured) — [PeoplePerHour](https://www.peopleperhour.com/hourlie/knowledge-graph-consultation-design-your-domain-structure/1131310)
- Fiverr/Upwork knowledge-graph gigs exist (e.g. [Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/fabisch_dev/create-knowledge-graphs-and-ontologies)); prices not captured. Knowledge Graph Conference community notes consultants suit small PoCs — [KGC](https://community.knowledgegraph.tech/x/jobs/hizlpm0q09ho/hiring-employees-vs-consultants-for-ontology-work)
- Large specialist firms (Semantic Arts, Enterprise Knowledge) serve enterprises; no public prices — [Semantic Arts](https://www.semanticarts.com/?p=9587)

### Inferences
- The niche of "Palantir-style ontology for small business" looks open but unproven; market evidence is for AI automation, so label the offer as such.

### Gaps
- No named solo founders with numbers selling ontology systems; "Palantir for small business" content found was an unsourced essay series. Local government and nonprofit buyers not covered.
